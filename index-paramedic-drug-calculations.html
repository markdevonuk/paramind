Why Drug Calculations Matter

Drug calculation errors are one of the most dangerous mistakes a clinician can make. For paramedics working under pressure in unpredictable environments, calculation accuracy isn't just an academic requirement — it directly affects patient safety.

The good news is that paramedic drug calculations follow a small number of straightforward formulae. With enough practice, they become second nature.

Always Estimate First

Before calculating, do a quick mental estimate of what a reasonable answer looks like. If your calculated answer is wildly different from your estimate, recheck your working. This simple habit catches the majority of calculation errors.

Core Calculation Formulae

Basic Dose Calculation

What you want ÷ What you've got × Volume

Example: You need to give 5mg of a drug. You have an ampoule of 10mg in 2ml.
5 ÷ 10 × 2 = 1ml

Weight-Based Dosing

Dose (mg/kg) × Patient weight (kg) = Total dose required

Then apply the basic formula to find the volume. Example: 0.1mg/kg adrenaline for a 70kg patient = 7mg total dose.

Infusion Rate

Volume (ml) ÷ Time (hours) = Rate (ml/hr)

Or for drops per minute: Volume (ml) × Drop factor ÷ Time (minutes)

Unit Conversions to Memorise

1g = 1000mg

Grams to milligrams

1mg = 1000 micrograms

Milligrams to micrograms

1 microgram = 1000 nanograms

For very small doses

1L = 1000ml

Litres to millilitres

Common Paramedic Drug Calculation Examples

Adrenaline (Anaphylaxis)

Adult dose: 0.5mg IM of 1:1000 adrenaline (1mg/ml). Volume required = 0.5mg ÷ 1mg/ml = 0.5ml. For IV cardiac arrest: 1mg of 1:10,000 (0.1mg/ml) = 10ml.

Morphine Sulphate

Typically 10mg in 10ml (1mg/ml). A 5mg dose = 5ml. Weight-based paediatric dosing: 0.1mg/kg — for a 30kg child, 3mg = 3ml.

Salbutamol Nebulisation

Standard dose 2.5mg–5mg. Typically supplied as 2.5mg/2.5ml or 5mg/2.5ml. Dilute to 4–6ml total with 0.9% sodium chloride.

Double Check Your Units

The most common source of errors is a unit mismatch — for example, mixing up mg and micrograms. Always convert everything to the same unit before calculating, and double-check your answer makes clinical sense.

Frequently Asked Questions

What drug calculations do paramedics need to know?

Paramedics need to calculate weight-based doses (mg/kg), infusion rates (ml/hr), dilution calculations, and unit conversions. Common examples include adrenaline in anaphylaxis, morphine in pain management, and fluid administration. The core formula is: dose required divided by dose available, multiplied by volume.

How do I improve my drug calculation accuracy?

Practise regularly with varied examples — not just the same type repeatedly. Always estimate your answer before calculating to catch obvious errors. Show your working clearly, especially in exams. Understanding why you are performing each step is more reliable than memorising formulae.

Are drug calculations tested in paramedic OSCEs?

Yes — drug calculation competency is assessed throughout paramedic training, including in OSCEs and placement sign-offs. Errors in drug calculations in real practice can be life-threatening, so accuracy under pressure is a core professional competency.
