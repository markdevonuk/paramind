Pharmacology: More Than Just Memorising Doses

Many students revise pharmacology by memorising drug lists. While knowing doses is essential, understanding the mechanism of each drug is far more valuable — it allows you to reason about contraindications, anticipate side effects, and understand why a drug works.

For example, knowing that adrenaline stimulates alpha and beta adrenoceptors explains why it increases heart rate (beta-1), causes bronchodilation (beta-2), and vasoconstricts (alpha-1) — and why it's the first-line drug in anaphylaxis and cardiac arrest.

Mechanism = Understanding

Learn drug class mechanisms first, then apply them to individual drugs. Beta-blockers block beta adrenoceptors — so they slow heart rate, reduce contractility, and can cause bronchospasm (contraindicated in asthma). Once you understand the class, individual drugs make sense.

Key Drug Categories for UK Paramedics

🫀 Cardiac Drugs

Aspirin, GTN, adenosine, amiodarone, atropine, adrenaline (cardiac arrest).

🫁 Respiratory Drugs

Salbutamol, ipratropium, hydrocortisone, adrenaline (anaphylaxis), oxygen.

💊 Analgesia

Morphine, paracetamol, ibuprofen, methoxyflurane (Penthrox), ketamine (specialist).

🧠 Neurological

Midazolam (seizures), dextrose/glucagon (hypoglycaemia), naloxone (opioid reversal).

🩸 Fluids

0.9% sodium chloride, Hartmann's solution, glucose solutions, colloids.

🔬 Hormones/Other

Glucagon, hydrocortisone, oxytocin (obstetric), tranexamic acid (trauma).

Pharmacology Revision Framework

For every drug, work through this framework:

1. Indication

What clinical situation is this drug used for? What is the evidence base? When would you consider it vs an alternative?

2. Mechanism

How does it work at a cellular or physiological level? Which receptors does it act on? This helps you understand and remember everything else.

3. Dose and Route

What is the standard adult dose? Is there a weight-based paediatric dose? Which routes are available (IV, IM, SC, oral, inhaled, intranasal)?

4. Contraindications and Cautions

What would make you not give this drug, or give it with extra care? Many contraindications make sense once you understand the mechanism.

5. Side Effects

What should you warn the patient about or monitor for after administration?

Frequently Asked Questions

What drugs do UK paramedics carry?

UK paramedics carry a range of medications depending on their scope of practice. Common drugs include adrenaline, aspirin, GTN, salbutamol, ipratropium, morphine, paracetamol, midazolam, naloxone, glucagon, and various fluids. NQPs and specialist practitioners may have extended formularies.

How should I revise paramedic pharmacology?

For each drug, learn the indication (when to use it), dose and route, mechanism of action, contraindications, cautions, and side effects. Understanding the mechanism helps you reason about contraindications rather than just memorising lists.

What pharmacology is tested in paramedic exams?

Exams typically test drug indications, doses, routes of administration, contraindications, and mechanisms. You may also be tested on drug interactions and the pharmacokinetics of drugs with narrow therapeutic windows. Calculations involving drug concentrations are commonly assessed.
